Fake Delivery Fee Scam: The €1.99 Rescheduling Trap

A fictional delivery notice uses a tiny rescheduling fee, a short deadline and a polished tracking interface to push visitors toward a payment page.

EDITORIAL NOTE
This is a fictional demonstration case created for the Scam Alert website. The names, messages and interfaces shown below are invented and are not presented as real-world allegations.

A small fee can make a suspicious request feel harmless

The fictional notice asks for only €1.99 to reschedule a delivery. The amount is intentionally small: it lowers the visitor’s resistance while the message creates a much larger sense of urgency around the parcel.

The link matters more than the price

A real delivery problem should be verifiable through the courier’s official website or app. We would inspect the destination domain, the redirect chain and the payment processor before entering any information.

What makes the flow suspicious

The combination of an unexpected delivery message, a deadline and a payment request supplied through the message is more important than the polished design. A familiar tracking layout is not proof that the sender is legitimate.
